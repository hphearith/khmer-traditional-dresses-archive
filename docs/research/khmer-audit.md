# Khmer string audit

- **Scope:** every Khmer string literal in `lib/uiText.js`, `data/garments.js`, `collection.config.js` and `lib/entry.js`: 215 distinct strings (224 occurrences). `lib/entry.js` has no Khmer string literals; its only Khmer character is the example "ក" in a code comment (line 98), which is not shown to users.
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Auditor:** an AI research agent. **Not a native Khmer speaker.** Under ADR 0003, every Khmer string still has to be read by a Khmer reader. This audit tells that reader where to look first; it does not replace them.
- **Method:**
  1. *Encoding.* A Node script (kept outside the repo) pulled out every string literal containing U+1780–U+17FF or U+19E0–U+19FF. For each one it checked: zero-width characters (U+200B, U+200C, U+200D, U+FEFF, U+2060); deprecated or discouraged code points (U+17A3, U+17A4, U+17B4, U+17B5, U+17D3, U+17D8); COENG not followed by a consonant or a subscriptable independent vowel; component order against the Unicode syllable grammar `B {R | C} {S {R}}* {{Z} V} {O} {S}`; two dependent vowels in one cluster; differences between NFC and the raw text; and a space before KHAN. It also listed every word that uses subscript TA (U+17D2 U+178F), subscript DA (U+17D2 U+178A), ឱ្យ or ឲ្យ, because those letters look the same on screen.
  2. *Spelling.* Each content word was looked up in Chuon Nath's dictionary (1967) through the SEAlang Library search (source filter "Chuon Nath"). Modern words that Chuon Nath does not have were looked up in Headley's *Cambodian-English Dictionary* (1997) through the same interface.
  3. *Meaning.* Each Khmer string was compared with its English pair, using the dictionary senses above.
  4. *Garment terms.* Checked against both dictionaries, the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts' 2026 guidance on Khmer traditional dress, and a museum collection record.
- **Verdict rule:** `OK` means three things hold: the encoding is clean, every key word is attested in a cited source (directly, or as a transparent compound of attested parts, noted where so), and the Khmer says what the English says. Anything that depends only on the auditor's own reading is `Needs native speaker`. Suggested fixes are marked *suggestion* and are not verified.

The mechanical scan found **no** zero-width characters, deprecated or discouraged code points, COENG errors, misplaced or doubled dependent vowels, or NFC differences anywhere in the four files.

**Top issues**

1. **Subscript TA written where Chuon Nath has subscript DA (37 strings).** ម្តង (28 strings), ប្តូរ (12), ម្តេច (1, `lib/uiText.js:40`) and ម្តាយ (1, `collection.config.js:26`) all use U+17D2 U+178F. Chuon Nath's headwords are ម្ដង, ប្ដូរ, ម្ដេច and ម្ដាយ, each with the note "ជើង ដ" (subscript ដ). Both forms look the same in most fonts, so no reader will notice. But a search for "ម្ដង" typed with DA will not find these strings, and the project is inconsistent with itself: `lib/uiText.js:55` writes ស្ដារ with DA, as Chuon Nath does. Words that really take TA (បន្ត, ប៉ុន្តែ, ចិត្ត, វត្ត, អចិន្ត្រៃយ៍) are correct. *Suggestion:* change the four words to DA everywhere at once, and confirm in the RAC app first.
2. **`data/garments.js:17` "សូត្រចងគៀត" (Chong Kiet).** Neither dictionary has ចងគៀត. Chuon Nath's គាត sense 2 is the silk that is tied into patterns to make sampot hol ("ចងគាត, ជ្រលក់គាត"), and it says គាធ is the clearer spelling. Headley gives គាត /kiet/ "silk threads tied together for dyeing" (variant គាធ). *Suggestion:* សូត្រចងគាធ or សូត្រចងគាត.
3. **`data/garments.js:65` and `:75` write សំលៀកបំពាក់.** Every other file writes សម្លៀកបំពាក់, and so do Chuon Nath and the Ministry of Culture's 2026 guidance. The record name "សំលៀកបំពាក់ការប្រពៃណី" (`:65`) may also not read as "Traditional Wedding Ensemble" (see its row).
4. **"You are no longer logged in" became "not yet logged in"** (`lib/uiText.js:59`, `:103`): 'អ្នកមិនទាន់ចូលគណនីទៀតទេ'.
5. **`data/garments.js:55` អាវចងពង់.** Neither dictionary has the term. Chuon Nath's definition of អាវ (cut and sewn, with collar, sleeves and buttons) contradicts the description (a wrapped chest tube). Ask the curator's source what this garment is called.
6. **Encoding order in កុម្ម៉ង់** (`lib/uiText.js:22`, `:40`). The consonant shifter comes after the subscript; the Unicode grammar puts it straight after the base. This is low impact (Headley's data stores it the same way), but it is the only syllable-order finding.
7. **Words that recur and need a native decision:** បណ្ណសារ for "archive" (13 strings; in neither dictionary); ការប្រមូល for "collection" (5; Chuon Nath's sense is the act of gathering); សម្ភារៈ for "materials" (5 strings, 7 places); and several literal, machine-translation-like phrasings (`lib/uiText.js:110, 170, 189, 192, 204, 243`).
8. **Garment material glosses translate only part of the English** (`data/garments.js:27, 37, 47`). The Khmer drops "twill/golden", "cotton & organza", and "pleated/sequins".

## For a native speaker

Questions that affect many strings. Answer these first.

1. **Subscript ត or ដ** in ម្តង / ប្តូរ / ម្តេច / ម្តាយ. Should they be changed to ម្ដង / ប្ដូរ / ម្ដេច / ម្ដាយ, as Chuon Nath has them? Please check the RAC Khmer Dictionary 2022 app, which this audit could not reach. For សេចក្តី, the sources disagree with themselves; the project currently uses TA.
2. **បណ្ណសារ for "archive"** (13 strings, including the site name 'បណ្ណសារខ្មែរមានជីវិត'). Is this the right word, and does 'មានជីវិត' come across as "living archive"?
3. **ការប្រមូល for "collection"** (a set of objects, as in a museum collection). Does it read as a collection, or as "the act of collecting"? If the latter, what word would you use?
4. **សម្ភារៈ for the "materials" of a dress** (silk, thread). Natural, or would វត្ថុធាតុ or សាច់ក្រណាត់ be better?
5. **រឿងរ៉ាវ for "Story".** Headley's first sense is "conflict, quarrel". Does the label read as "story"?
6. **Register of loan words:** អ៊ីមែល or សារអេឡិចត្រូនិក, ខូគី, អ៊ីនធឺណិត, ធីក, កាតាឡុក, ម៉ឺនុយ. Are these the spellings you would expect in a Khmer interface?
7. **A space before ។ after "@"** ('កុំវាយ @ ។'). Keep it or remove it?
8. **កុម្ម៉ង់ typing order.** On your keyboard, do you type the ៉ before or after the subscript ម? This decides whether to change the stored order.

Unicode says nothing about it, and it may be deliberate so the @ stands alone. A native speaker should decide. Otherwise the meaning matches. Chuon Nath: បន្ទាត់ 'line'. Headley: គូស 'to draw (a line)'.
- **ប្រើតែអក្សរអង់គ្លេស លេខ និងសញ្ញាគូសបន្ទាត់ក្រោមប៉ុណ្ណោះ។ កុំវាយ @ ។** (U382): There is a space before the KHAN (U+17D4) in '@ ។'. Unicode says nothing about it, and it may be deliberate so the @ stands alone. A native speaker should decide.
- **គ្មាន${km}ត្រូវនឹង “${shown}” ទេ។ សូមសាកល្បងឈ្មោះ ឬសម្ភារៈផ្សេង។** (U481): Chuon Nath defines សម្ភារ as 'components, equipment, necessities'. Headley gives សម្ភារៈ only in compounds (គ្រឿងសម្ភារៈ). For the materials of a dress (silk, thread), ask whether this reads naturally or whether a word like វត្ថុធាតុ or សាច់ក្រណាត់ fits better. Otherwise the meaning matches.
- **សូត្រមាស** (G7): Literally 'gold silk' (Chuon Nath: សូត្រ silk + មាស gold). The English says 'Khmer Golden Silk' (the golden silk of the native Cambodian silkworm), and the Khmer drops 'Khmer'. Ask whether សូត្រមាស is the term your source uses, or whether it could be read as gold-thread silk.
- **អាវចងពង់** (G55): 'អាវចងពង់' is in neither dictionary. Chuon Nath defines អាវ as a cut-and-sewn garment with collar, sleeves and buttons, but the description is a 'tube-like chest-wrap secured by folding and tucking'. Chuon Nath: ពង់ 'to arch, puff up, strut'. The term and the description seem to disagree: ask your source what this garment is called.
- **បណ្ណសារដែលកត់ត្រាការបង្កើតសម្លៀកបំពាក់ប្រពៃណីខ្មែរ ត្រូវបានបង្កើតឡើងសម្រាប់មុខវិជ្ជា ICT 340។** (C25): The meaning matches. បង្កើត appears twice ('creation' … 'was created'), with the passive 'ត្រូវបាន…ឡើង', which reads as translated. Chuon Nath's ត្រា entry supports កត់ត្រា, and Headley supports មុខវិជ្ជា 'subject'. បណ្ណសារ ('archive') is not in the accessible Chuon Nath data or in Headley 1997, so it cannot be confirmed here.

Uncertain points inside rows that have another verdict:

- **ស្ដារ…ឡើងវិញ for "restored"** (U55). Chuon Nath gives ស្ដារ only the senses "bail out, clear out, cleanse". Is "restore a draft" natural?
- **'វាលឿនពេក' for "too soon"** (U180). It literally says "too fast". Is that how a Khmer reader would say "it is too soon to change it again"?
- **'ប៉ាក់ឌិន'** (G47). The Ministry's guidance describes wedding sbai this way. Is ឌិន the word for the sequins in "Gold Thread & Sequins", and should it be added?
- **The "no longer logged in" fix** (U59, U103). Which wording is natural: 'អ្នកលែងនៅក្នុងគណនីទៀតហើយ', 'អ្នកបានចាកចេញពីគណនីហើយ', or something else?
- **Wedding ensemble name** (G65). Does 'សំលៀកបំពាក់ការប្រពៃណី' read as "traditional wedding clothes"? Would 'សម្លៀកបំពាក់អាពាហ៍ពិពាហ៍ប្រពៃណី' be better?
- **'សូត្រផាមួង', 'ក្រណាត់ប៉ាក់', 'ស្បៃប៉ាក់ខ្សែសយ'** (G27, G37, G47). Should the Khmer material names stay native names, or should they carry everything the English says?
